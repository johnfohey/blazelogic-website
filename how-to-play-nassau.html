Golf Bets -- Nassau · How to Play

Every hole has a price.

The complete Nassau rules — front nine, back nine, overall, and the three press modes — explained exactly as Golf Bets -- Nassau plays them. 2–4 players. No arguments.

The Basics

Three bets. One round.

A Nassau is three separate match-play bets in a single round. Each hole is won, lost, or tied — lowest score wins the hole.

Front 9

Holes 1–9 are their own bet. Win more holes than your opponent on the front, take the front.

Back 9

Holes 10–18 are their own bet. A fresh start at the turn — no matter what happened up front.

Overall

All 18 holes are their own bet. The full-round match, running alongside the nines.

Playing 9 holes? It's the front nine bet on its own. The app handles both.

Press Modes

Choose your press.

Your group picks one press mode before the round. This is where Nassau gets interesting.

No Presses

Keep it simple

Play the original Nassau bets with no additional presses.

Front, back, overall — and that's the whole story.

Auto Press

The comeback engine

When a player becomes 2 holes down, a new press automatically starts on the next eligible hole. The current bet stays active.

Fall behind and the app opens you a lifeline — while the original bet keeps running.

Hardcore Press

Double or nothing

When a player becomes 2 holes down, the current bet ends as a loss and a new bet starts All Square on the next eligible hole.

Go 2 down and the old bet is dead. New bet, even start, next hole. Not for the faint of heart.

Presses, Precisely

How many, and where.

The short version: fall 2 holes down and a press opens on the next hole — all square, same stake, fully automatic. Below is exactly how the app enforces it for each bet.

Front 9 · Back 9

Each nine runs its own press chain: the original bet, then Press #1, then Press #2 — then stop. There is no Press #3. A press always opens on a hole after the one where the 2-down happened, and at least one hole must be played after a press starts before the next one in the chain can open.

One bad start · two presses

Lose the first 2 holes and you're 2 down on two bets at once — the front nine and the overall count the same holes. So two presses open on hole 3: a front-nine press and an overall press, each all square at its own bet's stake. In Auto, both originals keep running (4 live bets). In Hardcore, both originals close as losses and the two replacements start fresh.

Overall · the full 18

The overall bet runs its own chain, separate from the nines — and it's the only bet that can press in both halves. Auto Press: up to 4 presses over 18 holes — 2 in the front half, 2 in the back half — with the original bet staying alive throughout. Hardcore Press: up to 2 presses total. One chain only: wherever the first 2-down happens sets it for the rest of the round, and the replacement opens on the next eligible hole, all square.

  • Auto vs. Hardcore, once more. Auto adds a press alongside the original bet. Hardcore ends the original bet as a loss and starts a replacement.
  • 9-hole rounds. Just the front-nine bet, with its own two-press chain.
  • Every pairing, every time. With 3–4 players, each head-to-head matchup tracks its own front, back, overall, and press chains independently.

Know the Rules

The fine print, minus the arguing.

2 strokes down ≠ 2 holes down

Presses trigger on match-play holes down — holes won versus holes lost — not total strokes. Lose two more holes than your opponent and you're 2 down, even if every hole was decided by a single stroke. A player can be 2 down as early as hole 2.

  • No accept / decline. Presses happen automatically. Nobody offers, nobody declines, nobody slow-plays the negotiation.
  • Same stake, every press. Every press uses the same amount as the original bet. No side deals on press size.
  • Tied holes change nothing. A tie doesn't move the match-play lead, so it can't trigger a press by itself.
  • Settlement is instant. Ahead = won, behind = lost, all square = push ($0). The app totals every bet and press the moment the round ends.
  • 2–4 players. Every pairing gets its own tracked match — head-to-head or full foursome chaos.
